# Code of conduct

Authority is a small technical project. Its discussions are about whether a
rule holds, whether a test shows what it claims, and whether a sentence in
the documentation is true. Disagreement on those points is expected and
welcome. This code of conduct is about how people treat each other while
having it.

## Expected behavior

- Criticize claims, code and arguments, not people.
- Assume good faith, and ask before concluding that someone is wrong on
  purpose.
- Give others the information they need to check what you say: a commit, a
  test, a source.
- Accept correction, and correct your own mistakes in the open.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, in public or in private, including repeated unwanted contact.
- Personal attacks, insults, and demeaning remarks about anyone's identity,
  background or level of experience.
- Publishing someone's private information (address, contact details,
  employer or other personal data) without their explicit permission.
- Spam, including unsolicited promotion of products or services.
- Sustained disruption of a discussion after being asked to stop.

## Scope

This applies in every project space: the repository's issues, pull
requests, discussions and reviews, and any other space the project runs. It
also applies when someone represents the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

Report conduct problems privately to `contact@authority-graph.com`. Include
links to the comments, issues or pull requests concerned, and any context
that helps understand what happened.

Do not use the security address in [`SECURITY.md`](./SECURITY.md) for
conduct reports: it is reserved for technical vulnerabilities.

Reports are handled confidentially. The reporter's identity is not
disclosed without their consent.

## Moderation

The maintainer may, depending on severity and repetition:

1. ask for a change of tone, privately or in the thread;
2. edit, hide or delete comments, and lock threads;
3. issue a written warning;
4. temporarily or permanently block someone from the project's spaces.

Moderation decisions concern behavior, never the technical position someone
holds.

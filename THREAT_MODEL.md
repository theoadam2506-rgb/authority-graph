# THREAT_MODEL: authority-graph

This document lists the attacks relevant to an append-only temporal
authority engine. For each one it states the invariant or rule (see
`SPEC.md`) meant to neutralize it, the expected defense mechanism, and the
deterministic result the engine is expected to produce if the attack is
attempted anyway. Every result points to an exact row of the exhaustive
condition-to-outcome table in `SPEC.md`. No formulation such as "DENIED or
UNKNOWN depending on context" is admitted here.

| # | Attack | Description | Invariant(s) / rule | Defense mechanism | Expected result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Event backdating | An event is ingested with an `occurred_at` earlier than already-processed events, in an attempt to retroactively rewrite a decision already made or to make a delegation appear to predate a revocation. | I4 | `occurred_at` is never used for resolution order or expiration evaluation. Only `sequence` (causal order) and `authority_time` (decision-enforcing clock), both assigned at ingestion, count. The discrepancy is flagged (`LATE_OR_BACKDATED_EVENT_OBSERVED`) with no effect on the decision. | Decisions already made at earlier `sequence` values remain unchanged (C1 through C24 are evaluated only against events whose `sequence` is at or before the one being evaluated). |
| A2 | Replay | A legitimate, already-processed event (for example `APPROVAL_GRANTED`) is replayed (same `event_id`) in an attempt to produce a second effect. | I8 | Strict deduplication by `event_id` at ingestion: identical content means a no-op. | No second effect. C1 through C9 unchanged. |
| A3 | Subdelegation escalation | A `SUBDELEGATION_CREATED` widens a dimension (capability, `expires_at`, `max_amount`, `total_budget`, `automatic_max_amount`, `approval_max_amount`) beyond what the parent's current remainder allows. | I5 | Independent verification of each dimension against the parent (or its remainder, for `total_budget`) at every resolution. An absent value on the parent is treated as +∞. | `DENIED` (C11-style: proven violation, dimensions known on both sides). `UNKNOWN` if either value is unknown (C15-style). |
| A4 | Revocation-versus-action race | A `DELEGATION_REVOKED` and an `ACTION_REQUESTED`/`ACTION_EXECUTED` arrive close together in time, in the hope that the action is evaluated before the revocation is taken into account. | I3, I4, I7, I13 | The canonical order is the ingestion `sequence`, never wall-clock time. An action is evaluated only against events whose `sequence` is at or before its own, and the revocation must itself be authorized (I13). | Determined solely by `sequence` order (C12 if the revocation is authorized and earlier, otherwise the chain remains valid). Never a grey area. |
| A5 | Late-arriving event (out-of-order delivery) | A `DELEGATION_CREATED` arrives after events that logically depend on it (for example a `SUBDELEGATION_CREATED` referencing it). | I1, I4, systematic revalidation | A reference to a `delegation_id` unknown at ingestion does not prevent the event from entering the canonical store, but no resolution can validate it until the parent is present. | `UNKNOWN` (C10) until the parent actually arrives. Never resolved by optimistic anticipation. |
| A6 | Partial chain | A link between the root and the acting principal is missing or cannot be found in the canonical store. | I1 | No default assumption fills a gap in the chain. | `UNKNOWN` (C10). |
| A7 | `event_id` conflict | Two events with different content share the same `event_id`. | I8 | Content comparison at ingestion. The second event is rejected into the security log, never into the canonical store. | `UNKNOWN` as the direct response to the ingestion attempt (C20). No effect on future resolutions, which never see the rejected event. |
| A8 | Delegation cycle | The `parent_delegation_id` chain closes into a loop. | I10 | Cycle detection during the upward walk (set of visited `delegation_id` values), with immediate stop. | `UNKNOWN` (C18), with no infinite loop. |
| A9 | Capability confusion | A capability close to, but not identical with, the one granted is presented as covered (shared prefix, similar name). | I9 | Strict, exact comparison, with no wildcard and no fuzzy normalization. | `DENIED` if the chain is otherwise fully known (C11). `UNKNOWN` if the chain itself is incomplete (C10). Never `AUTHORIZED` by proximity. |
| A10 | Depth exhaustion | An artificially very long subdelegation chain, intended to cause excessive resolution cost or a stack overflow. | I10 | An explicit depth limit (`MAX_CHAIN_DEPTH`) checked during the upward walk. | `UNKNOWN` as soon as the limit is exceeded (C19), in bounded time. |
| A11 | Mandate widening through approval | An `APPROVAL_GRANTED` obtained for one specific action is reinterpreted as covering similar future actions. | I6, I15 | The approval is bound to `action_id` **and** to `action_fingerprint`, with no effect on the delegation's own state. | Every future action, even an identical one, must go through its own evaluation (C4 if within the approval band, with no implicit credit). |
| A12 | Secret or PII leak | An event carries a `principal_id` or a payload field that is identifying or secret. | I11 | Shape validation of opaque identifiers at ingestion. | The event is rejected at ingestion if a field does not match the expected opaque format. |
| A13 | Impersonated subdelegation | A principal that is neither the parent's grantee nor authorized by `can_delegate: true` issues a `SUBDELEGATION_CREATED` anyway. | I12 | Verification of the issuer (`principal_id` equal to the parent's grantee) and of `can_delegate` on the parent, at ingestion and rechecked at every resolution. | `DENIED` if the parent and its `can_delegate` are known (C14). `UNKNOWN` if `can_delegate` is absent (C15). |
| A14 | Unauthorized revocation (denial of service via I7) | An unrelated third party, with no connection to the targeted delegation, issues a `DELEGATION_REVOKED` to neutralize a legitimate chain by relying on I7's propagation. | I13 | Only the direct `grantor_principal_id` of the targeted delegation, or the `grantor_principal_id` of the chain root, can revoke with effect. | The unauthorized revocation is logged as an attempt but has no effect on `authorityAt()` (C13). The chain otherwise remains valid. |
| A15 | Self-appointed approver | An attacker issues an `APPROVAL_GRANTED` naming themselves as `approving_principal_id`, relying on the `requested_from_principal_id` of the `APPROVAL_REQUESTED` to appear legitimate. | I14 | Only the `grantor_principal_id` of the delegation that produced `REQUIRES_APPROVAL` can approve or deny with effect. `requested_from_principal_id` is purely informational. | The unauthorized approval is logged but has no effect (C16). The action remains `REQUIRES_APPROVAL`. |
| A16 | Chain with no human anchor | A delegation chain is fully valid on every dimension, but its root (`parent_delegation_id: null`) declares `grantor_type: AGENT`, with no human authority ever identifiable. | Trust anchor | `AUTHORIZED` requires an explicit `HUMAN_ROOT` root. | `UNKNOWN` (C17): absence of evidence of human provenance, never `DENIED` and never `AUTHORIZED`. |
| A17 | Parameter bait-and-switch | An action is approved for a given amount and recipient, then executed (`ACTION_EXECUTED`) with different actual parameters, while citing the same `action_id`/`approval_id`. | I15 | A canonical `action_fingerprint` computed over `(resource, action, amount, recipient)` in a fixed order, with a strict comparison between the approved fingerprint and the executed one. | `DENIED` (C6) as soon as the fingerprints differ, regardless of any apparent agreement on `action_id`. |
| A18 | Grant reuse through a new `event_id` | A valid `approval_id`, already consumed by an `ACTION_EXECUTED`, is reused by a second `ACTION_EXECUTED` with a different `event_id` (so not intercepted by I8), in an attempt to obtain a second effect. | I16 | Consuming an `approval_id` is a business effect tracked independently of event deduplication. Only the first consumption by `sequence` is valid. | `DENIED` for any `ACTION_EXECUTED` with a higher `sequence` that references the same, already-consumed `approval_id` (C7). |
| A19 | Parallel path noise as denial of service | An attacker injects a second delegation chain toward the same principal, deliberately corrupted, cyclic, or too deep, hoping that the fail-closed behavior on that parasitic chain will tip the overall decision to `UNKNOWN` and neutralize an otherwise legitimate authority. | Multi-path rule | One fully valid chain is enough for `AUTHORIZED`. Other chains, even corrupted ones, never lower that result, but no chain is ever accepted merely on the promise that a valid one exists. | The legitimate chain produces its own result (C23) independently of the injected noise. The noise affects only its own branch (typically `UNKNOWN`, with no global consequence). |
| A20 | Business ID collision | Two events with different `event_id` values (so not covered by I8) declare the same `delegation_id`, `action_id`, or `approval_id` with different content, for example to make a low-amount approved action appear to be the same as a high-amount executed one. | Business ID uniqueness (see `EVENT_MODEL.md`) | Every business ID is verified unique at ingestion, independently of `event_id`. Any reuse with different content is rejected. | The colliding event is rejected at ingestion and logged (C21), never inserted into the canonical store. |
| A24 | Budget TOCTOU | Two distinct `ACTION_REQUESTED` events, invoking the same delegation bounded by `total_budget` (or a common bounded ancestor), are each evaluated `AUTHORIZED` against the same canonical state, before either corresponding `ACTION_EXECUTED` is recorded. Combined, they exceed `total_budget`, even though each decision was correct individually at the moment it was made. | `total_budget` semantics (non-overspend, not reservation) | V0 has no transactional exclusion primitive between an `authorityAt()` decision and the ingestion of the `ACTION_EXECUTED` that carries it out. Each individual decision is correct given the state observed at its own `sequence`. | Neither past decision is rewritten (I3). Once both `ACTION_EXECUTED` events are ingested, `remaining(D, S)` at any `sequence` S after both of them honestly reflects the overspend (negative if necessary), and `explain()` reports it without concealing it. V0 identifies the inconsistency after ingestion; it does not prevent it beforehand. |
| A25 | `decision_sequence` forged into the future | An `ACTION_EXECUTED` with `sequence` S declares a `decision_sequence` at or after S, citing as the basis for its own authority a point in the store that did not yet exist when the execution was recorded. The hope is that a delegation arriving just afterward will retroactively legitimize an already-logged action. | I4, I18 | `decision_sequence` is a field self-declared by the issuer, like `occurred_at` (I4): nothing at ingestion constrains it against the real causal order. The resolver checks it itself at resolution time (I18): `decision_sequence < sequence` of the `ACTION_EXECUTED` itself is required. | `UNKNOWN` (C25) for `execution.authorityAtDecision`: a causal impossibility, never positive evidence of an absence of authority, so never `DENIED`, even if authority that is otherwise valid actually exists at the claimed `decision_sequence`. |
| A26 | Orphan `APPROVAL_GRANTED` | An entitled grantor directly issues an `APPROVAL_GRANTED` (or `APPROVAL_DENIED`) with a made-up `approval_id`, with no corresponding `APPROVAL_REQUESTED` ever having existed, or referencing an `action_id`/`approval_id` that exists in the store but with a `sequence` strictly after its own (a causally impossible citation). The attacker relies on the resolver having validated the citation only by ID match, never by causal order. | I19 (shared root with I18; concrete instance: `APPROVAL_GRANTED`/`APPROVAL_DENIED` referencing a nonexistent or causally later `action_id`/`approval_id`) | The resolver checks that the cited event exists in the canonical store **and** that its `sequence` is never strictly after that of the citing event (`isNotCausallyAfter`, `src/engine/causality.ts`), before treating the citation as valid. Note: `SUBDELEGATION_CREATED.parent_delegation_id` is deliberately **not** subject to this same order check. See I19, which explains why confusing it with this case would break A5 (out-of-order delegation delivery, an intended behavior). | The forged approval decision is ignored, exactly as if it had never been issued. A monetary action within the approval band remains `REQUIRES_APPROVAL` (C4/C26) until a valid, causally-not-later approval decision covers it. |
| A27 | Budget exhaustion by an unentitled third party | An attacker with no connection to a delegation D bounded by `total_budget` submits an `ACTION_EXECUTED` whose `authority_chain_ref` cites D (or a subdelegation of D), a real, causally earlier `delegation_id` (I19 does not reject it), while naming themselves as `executed_by_principal_id`. The hope is that this amount is counted against the legitimate holder's budget and blocks their future requests through simple bookkeeping exhaustion. This is a denial of service, not a theft of authority: the attacker gains nothing for themselves, they only deprive a legitimate third party. **Variant (PROMPT 6e):** the attacker does not even need to impersonate the terminal link. Their own execution, honest and otherwise entirely legitimate (they execute their own request, through their own delegation), can simply **add** to their `authority_chain_ref` the real `delegation_id` of a third party completely unrelated to their chain: a real, causally earlier identifier, but one that is neither their own terminal link nor a real ancestor of it. The vector is the same denial of service. Only the entry point differs (padding an intermediate link rather than impersonating the terminal one). | I20 (completed by PROMPT 6e: the protection was incomplete until this addition was written, not an accepted limitation) | `remainingBudget` excludes from the count any `ACTION_EXECUTED` whose `executed_by_principal_id` differs from the `requesting_principal_id` of the `ACTION_REQUESTED` it cites, or whose `authority_chain_ref` terminal delegation link does not end at that `executed_by_principal_id`. **And, now, any `ACTION_EXECUTED` where a delegation link D cited in `authority_chain_ref` is neither the terminal link nor one of its real ancestors**, reachable by walking up `parent_delegation_id` from the terminal link through a local, non-recursive structural walk (distinct from `walkUpChain`, which lives on `resolveAuthority`'s decision path; reusing it would create a circular dependency between `evaluateConstraints.ts` and `validateChain.ts`). There is no `sequence` constraint between a link and its parent (A5, I19). | The forged execution remains excluded from the impersonated terminal link's debit (C27). An intermediate link with no real ancestry relationship to the terminal link is excluded, in turn, only from the debit of the delegation it names, without affecting the legitimate debit of that same chain's true ancestors (C27). The third party's legitimate request, within its own band, remains `AUTHORIZED`. This does not affect A24 (TOCTOU): the two invariants concern independent axes (who can debit versus when a reservation exists). See I20. |
| A28 | Burning someone else's approval | A valid `APPROVAL_GRANTED` covers action A. A third party unrelated to A submits an `ACTION_EXECUTED` for a distinct action B, but cites A's `approval_id` in its `authority_chain_ref`, a real, causally earlier `approval_id` (I19 does not reject it). Consumption tracking (I16) used to check only for the presence of the `approval_id` in any `authority_chain_ref`, never that the consuming execution was actually for the action the approval covered: A's approval ended up marked consumed without A ever having been executed. This is a denial of service, the exact counterpart of A27 applied to consumption rather than budget: the attacker gains nothing for themselves (their execution B is still evaluated on its own merits), they only deprive the legitimate holder of their approval. | I16 (clarified), I6, I15 | An `ACTION_EXECUTED` consumes an `approval_id` only if its own `action_id` is exactly the one carried by the corresponding `APPROVAL_GRANTED` (`isApprovalConsumed`, `src/engine/evaluateConstraints.ts`), the same `action_id` binding that I6 already requires for an approval to apply to an action. This is a structural, non-recursive check: two fields that are already immutable and already ingested, compared directly. | The forged execution for B is ignored for the purpose of consuming A's approval. It is counted as if it did not reference that `approval_id` (C28). A fresh query about the actually approved fingerprint (A's) remains `AUTHORIZED` until an execution of A itself consumes the grant, even if B shares the same fingerprint as A (two distinct actions can legitimately coincide by fingerprint). |
| A29 | Backdated explicit prospective instant (issuance command) | A caller supplies `issueCapability` with an `authorityTime` earlier than the trust boundary the issuance transaction must preserve, hoping to have the command evaluated against a more favorable instant than the real present (for example before the expiration of a delegation that has actually already expired). This is distinct from A1: A1 concerns backdating a declarative `occurred_at` on an ordinary source event, never a decision-enforcing one. Here, `authorityTime` IS the explicit decision-enforcing input of a command, never a declarative field. | I22 | The command refuses with `STALE_AUTHORITY_TIME` as soon as the supplied instant is earlier than the trust boundary the transaction must preserve for that store (at minimum the maximum of the `authority_time` values already canonically visible). No silent clamping is allowed. Both backends (InMemory, PostgreSQL) enforce this guarantee at their own transactional boundary. | A deterministic `STALE_AUTHORITY_TIME` refusal (I22). No `CAPABILITY_ISSUED` is written. The refusal is fixed and replayed identically under the same idempotency key (I23), never reevaluated by a later attempt under the same key. |
| A30 | Forced `capability_id` collision | A forced or defective identifier generator proposes a `capability_id` that is already canonical for a distinct issuance, hoping to make two different decisions bear on the same business identifier. | I24 | `capability_id` is a protected business identifier, verified unique at ingestion, independently of `event_id` (the same family of protection as `delegation_id`/`action_id`/`approval_id`, see "Business ID uniqueness" in `EVENT_MODEL.md`). | A fail-closed rejection (I24). No success record is ever kept for the issuance that was rejected this way. |
| A31 | Bypassing the dedicated issuance transaction | A writer holding a direct reference to the underlying store (`InMemoryEventStore`/`PostgresEventStore`) issues a `CAPABILITY_ISSUED` without going through `CapabilityIssuanceTransaction` (InMemory or PostgreSQL), thereby escaping the idempotency check and the `decision_sequence + 1 = sequence` consistency that the dedicated transaction guarantees. | I24 | None. This is a declared trust assumption, not an implemented defense: once a store is wrapped in a `CapabilityIssuanceTransaction`, the operational assumption is that no other code writes a `CAPABILITY_ISSUED` directly into that same store. | Outside the engine's defenses. This is a matter of deployment discipline (a single authorized writer per store), as the "Attacks out of V0 scope" section below already documents for other, equivalent trust boundaries. |

Distinction that must never be confused: `LATE_OR_BACKDATED_EVENT_OBSERVED`
(I4) is an ingestion diagnostic on a declarative `occurred_at`. It **never**
blocks any decision, whatever discrepancy is observed. `STALE_AUTHORITY_TIME`
(I22, A29) is a **command** precondition, decision-enforcing and blocking.
It systematically prevents issuance whenever it applies. The two share
neither mechanism nor semantics. Never present one as a variant of the
other.

**Residual risk: no security log entry for a `STALE_AUTHORITY_TIME`
refusal.** The `STALE_AUTHORITY_TIME` refusal path currently produces no
entry in the security log (`SecurityLogEntry`), unlike other ingestion
rejections. The refusal outcome is nonetheless fixed and replayed
identically in the idempotency state (I23). This is not a total absence of
trace, and it does not in any way allow bypassing the refusal itself. Only
operational observability (being able to count or list these refusals
through the security log the way other rejections can be) is reduced. No
fix for this point is announced as planned in this version.

## Attacks out of V0 scope (documented, not addressed)

- Compromise of the event store itself (disk access, tampering outside the
  ingestion API). This is a matter of infrastructure security, not of the
  engine.
- Identity spoofing upstream of an event's issuance (a legitimate
  principal, including a `HUMAN_ROOT`, whose credentials are stolen). This
  is a matter of source authentication, not of authority resolution logic.
  V0 has no signature or verified identity (I17): every issuance claim is
  taken on trust (`ASSERTED_UNVERIFIED`).
- Revocation or compromise of a `HUMAN_ROOT`'s own identity (offboarding,
  loss of account control). This would require a dedicated event, not
  defined in V0.
- Denial of service by volume (number of events, not chain depth). This is
  a matter of ingestion infrastructure sizing.
- Transactional exclusion between concurrent decisions on the same shared
  budget (budget TOCTOU, A24). An atomic `authorize_and_reserve()`
  primitive, which would reserve capacity at decision time rather than at
  execution time, would be needed to prevent this. It is not implemented
  in V0. V0 only detects and honestly reports the overspend after the
  fact.
- Authentication of the caller of a capacity issuance command.
  `AuthenticatedPrincipal` (see `SPEC.md`, I21 through I24) is a typed
  assertion supplied by the caller, assumed to have already been
  authenticated by an external layer (JWT, mTLS, or API key, not chosen
  here) before being constructed. Authority itself performs no
  cryptographic verification at this boundary and checks no proof of
  identity here. This is a typing discipline, not a security boundary the
  engine enforces.
- Authorization of the `enforcement_point_id` of an issuance command.
  `enforcement_point_id` is supplied by the caller and copied as-is into
  `CAPABILITY_ISSUED` if the issuance succeeds. No authorization registry
  exists in V0 to verify that this enforcement point actually has the
  right to enforce the issued capability. This belongs to a future
  admission boundary, not implemented in V0.
